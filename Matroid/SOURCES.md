# External formal sources

## N037: labeled single-element extensions and modular cuts (current five-batch invocation)

- Source: James Oxley, *Matroid Theory*, second edition (2011), Lemma 7.2.2 and Theorem 7.2.3, printed pp. 266–268, [PDF](https://www.math.ens.psl.eu/~benoist/refs/Oxley.pdf). The lemma proves that every single-element extension determines a modular cut. The theorem proves that every modular cut determines a unique extension on the fixed ground set `E ∪ {e}` and gives its rank on both old sets and sets containing `e`. Oxley explicitly includes the empty cut (coloop) and all-flat cut (loop).
- Exact statement comparison: `IsSingleElementExtension N M τ` asserts `τ ∉ N.E`, `M.E = insert τ N.E`, and `M ↾ N.E = N`. `IsModularCut` asserts precisely that members are flats, are upward closed among flats, and are closed under intersection of modular pairs. Oxley's source characterizes selected flats by `F ∪ {τ}` being a flat of the extension of the same rank as `F`. Since `F` is already a flat of `N` and the restriction of `M` is `N`, this is equivalent to `τ ∈ M.closure F`: the old part of `M.closure F` is exactly `N.closure F = F`. The source uses no simplicity, representability, or nonloop condition, and fixed `τ` preserves literal labels. The Lean `[N.Finite]` hypothesis is a specialization of the finite-matroid setting in the manuscript and source.
- The pinned mathlib matroid tree has no modular-cut classification theorem. `External.extensionCut_isModularCut` isolates Lemma 7.2.2, and `External.modularCut_unique_extension` isolates Theorem 7.2.3, each with one `sorry`. The public `extension_modularCut` and `modularCut_extension_unique` expose the exact manuscript closure-cut wording; their `#print axioms` includes `sorryAx`. N037 is `CITED_ASSUMPTION`. N035, including the necessary nonempty-family qualification for intersections, is proved locally and has no external premise.

- Source: James Oxley, *Matroid Theory*, second edition (2011), Lemma 6.6.9, printed p. 210, [PDF](https://www.math.ens.psl.eu/~benoist/refs/Oxley.pdf). The `mapEmbedding` equations in the cited premises state this relabeling explicitly and retain the original deletion/contraction labels. There are no additional regularity, orientation, or port hypotheses in the source or Lean statements.
